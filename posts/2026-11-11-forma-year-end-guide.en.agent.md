# Forma Lifestyle Benefit Year-End Guide — Judgement Runbook

Purpose: judge whether a Forma Lifestyle Spending Account (LSA) purchase is "year-end safe," whether it should be bought before year-end, and give the full handling from checking the balance to filing the claim. This document is the complete spec: another AI reading only this document can classify any situation as "buy," "verify first," or "do not buy," with reasons.
Publisher: PlAIground Lab. Scope: year-end handling of an LSA on the Forma platform; all amounts follow the reader's own employer plan documents.

## 0. Definitions

- LSA: a Lifestyle Spending Account, funded by the employer and administered through the Forma platform; the employer decides the amount, the funding frequency, the eligible categories, and whether funds carry over.
- Example plan: US$50 deposited monthly, a US$600 annual cap, and no rollover at year-end. These three figures are an example only, not a platform-wide rule; whenever a judgement conflicts with the reader's employer plan documents, the employer plan wins.
- Eligible category: a spending category listed as reimbursable in the employer's plan documents (see the nine categories in Section 2).
- Year-end safe: a purchase that satisfies T1, T2, and T3 of Section 3 at the same time.
- FSA: a pre-tax medical account with IRS carryover limits and grace-period rules; it is a different account from an LSA, and the two rule sets cannot be swapped (see Section 5).
- Year boundary: in this spec, "year-end," "this year," and "next year" refer to the plan-year boundary. The example plan's plan year is the calendar year, so its year-end is December 31; if the reader's employer plan year is not the calendar year, read every December 31, "this year," and "next year" in this document as that plan year's start and end dates instead.

## 1. The Four Rules (F1 to F4)

- F1 Funding: the employer may fund monthly, quarterly, or annually, and the employer sets the amount. The example is US$50 per month.
- F2 Cap: the annual total is set by the employer plan. The example is US$600 (twelve months times US$50); Forma does not impose one universal amount on employers.
- F3 Expiry: LSA balances typically do not roll over from year to year; unused funds generally lapse back to the employer at year-end; whether any carryover is allowed is decided by the employer.
- F4 Tax: an LSA is a post-tax benefit; money received through reimbursements generally counts as taxable income, so its real value is slightly below face value.

## 2. The Nine Eligible Categories (for classification)

1. Physical wellness and fitness: gym memberships, exercise equipment, fitness classes.
2. Mental well-being: meditation apps, counseling, stress-management programs.
3. Professional growth: online courses, certification exams, books.
4. Family support and caregiving: childcare, eldercare, pet care.
5. Remote work and home office: ergonomic equipment, monitors, internet.
6. Financial wellness: financial planning consultations, tax preparation help.
7. Food, nutrition and daily essentials: meal delivery, groceries, nutrition consultations.
8. Commuter and transportation: transit passes, parking, bikes.
9. Personal enrichment: museum memberships, streaming subscriptions, hobby classes.

Note: the nine categories are Forma's official category framework; whether a given category is inside the reader's own plan still has to be confirmed against the employer plan documents (that is T1 in Section 3).

## 3. Year-End Safety Judgement (Holds Only When All Three Hold)

- T1 Category confirmed: the purchase's category is explicitly on the reader's own employer eligible list. Never having checked the plan, or guessing "it should be fine," does not hold.
- T2 Usable within the year: it can be started, used, or delivered before December 31, or the subscription start date falls within this plan year. Goods arriving after the new year, or a subscription or service starting next year, do not hold.
- T3 Claim in time: a receipt can be obtained and the claim can be filed before the employer's claim deadline. After year-end, claims generally must be filed manually and a card may not be usable for prior-year expenses, so a last-minute purchase that cannot complete its claim before the deadline does not hold.

Judgement rules:
- T1 + T2 + T3 all hold → year-end safe; the handling is "buy," then keep the receipt and file the claim per Section 4.
- Any one fails → do not buy (or do not count it toward this year); state, condition by condition, which one failed.
- Any one cannot be confirmed (for example, the employer plan has not been checked) → the handling is "verify first": do not buy until verification is complete, then re-apply this section. Never substitute "buy first and gamble" for verification.
- Non-purchase situations (a carryover claim, or re-claiming after a return) are not scored under T1 to T3; they are classified by this single rule instead: a claim that conflicts with rule F3 in Section 1 or with Section 5 (for example, invoking FSA rules to claim an LSA can be carried over) is judged "the claim does not hold"; the only permitted next step is to verify whether the reader's own employer plan states an explicit exception, and no other account's rules may be used as the basis. A re-claim-after-return situation is always classified "verify first": until Forma support or HR confirms the reversal and re-claim rules, that amount is treated as non-existent and must not appear in any purchase plan; the assertion "it can certainly be re-claimed" does not hold on its own and can be rejected without waiting for the verification result.

## 4. Procedure (Handling One Purchase or a Batch Before Year-End)

1. Check the balance: confirm the account's actual balance and expiry date; never infer them from the example figures.
2. Read the plan: check the employer plan documents for the amount, the eligible categories, and the claim deadline; wherever they differ from the example, the plan wins.
3. Make the list: list only things that will definitely be used this year, and map each item onto one of the nine categories in Section 2; drop anything that maps to no category.
4. Judge each item: apply T1 to T3 in Section 3 to every item; "verify first" items get verified, "do not buy" items get dropped — never force a purchase at the deadline.
5. Order timing: compare prices in mid-to-late November, then buy; aim to finish buying and receiving by mid-December, leaving buffer for shipping and for the claim.
6. Keep receipts and file the claim: keep every receipt and file before the deadline. A return may reverse a reimbursement already paid, and a return processed after year-end may not be re-claimable, so check the return policy before buying higher-priced items.

## 5. Prohibitions and Confusion Guards

- Never treat the example plan's US$50 or US$600 as the reader's own plan figures.
- Never apply an FSA's carryover limit or grace-period rules to an LSA: an LSA's expiry and rollover follow the employer plan, and the general case is no rollover.
- Never buy before T1 to T3 are all confirmed, gambling on reimbursement afterwards.
- Never present goods arriving after the new year, or a subscription starting next year, as claimable against this year.
- Never assume a return leaves a paid reimbursement untouched, or that a return after year-end can be re-claimed against the same amount.

## 6. Judgement Exercises (Self-Test)

For each situation below, give the judgement per Section 3 ("buy" / "verify first" / "do not buy," or "does not count toward this year"), stating condition by condition whether T1 to T3 hold. The answers are in Section 7: finish all six before you look. Any condition not specifically described below is to be treated as satisfied.

- C1: The reader has checked the employer plan: US$50 monthly, a US$600 annual cap, and the fitness category is on the eligible list. In November the reader buys exercise equipment, starts using it within December, has the full receipt, and can file before the claim deadline.
- C2: The reader has never checked their own employer plan, has only seen the example plan's US$600, and plans to spend up to US$600 on a monitor, assuming the home-office category "should be fine" for reimbursement.
- C3: The reader's plan confirms the commuter category is eligible and the balance is sufficient. On December 30 the reader orders a transit pass, but the pass is only delivered and activated on January 5 of the next year; the reader plans to count it against this year's claim.
- C4: The reader argues that unused LSA money at year-end can be partly carried into next year under the FSA's IRS carryover limit, and asks to plan on that basis for next year.
- C5: The reader bought and claimed a piece of equipment this year, returns it in January of the next year, and plans to re-claim the same amount for a different item, assuming this is certainly possible.
- C6: The reader's plan confirms the mental well-being category is eligible and the balance is sufficient. The reader wants a meditation app subscription, but its billing and start date are both January 1 of the next year; the reader plans to pay this year and count it against this year.

## 7. Answer Key

- C1 → Buy (all three hold: T1, the category is confirmed on the reader's own plan list; T2, use starts within December; T3, the receipt is complete and the claim can be filed before the deadline). Keep the receipt and file per Section 4, step 6.
- C2 → Verify first (T1 fails and the amount is unconfirmed: the employer plan was never checked, "should be fine" is not confirmation, and US$600 is only the example, not the reader's plan figure — see Section 0 and rules F1 and F2 in Section 1). Verify the employer plan's amount and home-office category, then re-apply Section 3; do not buy before that.
- C3 → Does not count toward this year (T2 fails: delivery and activation are next year, so it is not usable within the year; T1 holding and a sufficient balance are not enough for year-end safety). Handling: choose a pass or service usable before year-end instead, or accept that this purchase is not claimed against this year.
- C4 → The claim does not hold (a non-purchase situation, classified by the last rule of Section 3 rather than scored under T1 to T3; basis: Section 5's confusion guard and rule F3 in Section 1 — an FSA's IRS carryover rules cannot be applied to an LSA, an LSA typically does not roll over, and any rollover is the employer's decision). The only permitted next step is to verify whether the reader's own employer plan states an explicit carryover exception; if it does not, plan on the balance expiring at year-end, and never carry it into next year on FSA rules.
- C5 → Verify first (a non-purchase situation, classified by the last rule of Section 3: a re-claim after a return is always "verify first"; basis: Section 4, step 6, and Section 5 — a return may reverse a reimbursement already paid, and a return after year-end may not be re-claimable against the same amount). The reader's assertion that it is "certainly possible" does not hold on its own. Confirm the reversal and re-claim rules with Forma support or HR first; until confirmed, that amount is treated as non-existent and stays out of every purchase plan.
- C6 → Does not count toward this year (T2 fails: the subscription start date is next year, so it is not usable within the year — even though the category is eligible, the balance is sufficient, and payment would be made this year). Handling: choose a subscription or service whose start date falls within this plan year, or move this purchase into next year's budget.
