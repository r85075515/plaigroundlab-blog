The Best-Looking Model Was the Least Obedient: Three SDXL Models, Same Prompts, Tested Head-to-Head

For your AI: this post ships with a clean-Markdown agent edition — read it and follow along: English · 中文版

Opening: The Story, Written for Humans

Are community models really stronger than the stock base model? We put three SDXL models into the same examination room under one unified specification: the stock SDXL base 1.0, the community favourite Juggernaut XL v9, and RealVisXL V5.0, which specialises in realism — four test prompts, the same seed, the same parameters, nobody allowed to run on their home settings.

The conclusion first, and it is a little surprising: the composite winner is Juggernaut XL v9 (8.25), the stock base comes second (7.75), and RealVisXL V5.0 third (7.5) — but on the one test that measures obedience, the only model that got everything right was the stock base. The deciding prompt asked for a purple elephant wearing a yellow-and-black striped party hat, with a red balloon, standing in a snowy pine forest. Both community models, independently, changed the red balloon to yellow — presumably because yellow matched the striped hat better. Silently "fixing" your strange requirement in the name of a better-looking picture is a hard flaw for creators who need precise control: you do not want the version it thinks looks good; you want the version you ordered.

On image quality, the community lead is just as real. Juggernaut's portrait close-up is the most polished — makeup, hair strands, and gaze all land; RealVis has the most natural skin texture, the closest to a real photograph; in the full-body rainy-night scene Juggernaut is the most cinematic and RealVis the most like a street photograph. The stock base even slipped on the portrait test itself: the prompt said shoulder-length hair, and it delivered long hair. So the real lesson of this match is not "which model is strongest" but that image quality and obedience are two separate axes, and choosing a model means first knowing which axis you are buying — the highest composite scorer may rank last on exactly the dimension you care about most. The second half of this post writes the examination specification, the full scores, the per-test observations, and the selection judgement down hard, so anyone — or any AI — handed a selection scenario can produce exactly one answer.

The Complete Runbook Follows

1. Examination Specification (Fairness Conditions)

  • Platform: a free-tier cloud notebook environment with a T4-class GPU. Speed figures are comparable only on the same platform.
  • One unified specification for every model: seed 42, 30 steps, cfg 7, 1024×1024, and the same negative prompt set. If any model switches to its own customary parameters, this round's ranking no longer applies (see Section 5).
  • Four test prompts: Q1 portrait close-up (a 29-year-old Japanese woman, shoulder-length wavy hair); Q2 full-body scene (a rainy Tokyo street at night, trench coat and umbrella); Q3 pure scene (a lakeside mountain cabin at dusk); Q4 instruction fidelity (a purple elephant + a yellow-and-black striped party hat + a red balloon, in a snowy pine forest).
  • Scoring method: a single blind reviewer — the reviewer sees the images without filename order. The scores are one reviewer's result, not a multi-judge panel consensus (see Section 5).

2. Score Table and Composite Rule

Each of the four dimensions is scored out of 10; the composite is the unweighted mean of the four:

Model Portrait Full-body / Scene Instruction fidelity Speed Composite
Juggernaut XL v9 9 9 7 8 8.25
SDXL base 1.0 7 7 9 8 7.75
RealVisXL V5.0 8 9 7 6 7.5
  • The "Full-body / Scene" column combines performance on Q2 and Q3; the "Instruction fidelity" column refers specifically to attribute accuracy on Q4 (whether each specified colour and object is right). The hair-length miss in Q1 is a qualitative observation and is not folded into that score (see N1 in Section 3).
  • Speed, split open: generation time is in the same class for all three, 26–28 seconds per image; the gap is in loading (including first download) — base 104 seconds, Juggernaut 109 seconds, RealVis 206 seconds (a larger model repository). RealVis's speed score of 6 is deducted for loading, not generation.

3. Per-Test Observations (N)

  • N1 Portrait (Q1): Juggernaut is the most polished (makeup, hair strands, and gaze all land); RealVis has the most natural skin, the closest to a real photograph; base rendered the explicitly specified shoulder-length hair as long hair — obedience leaked already on the portrait test. This is a qualitative observation; the instruction-fidelity score remains anchored to Q4.
  • N2 Full-body scene (Q2): Juggernaut is the most cinematic (rainy-night neon, wet-ground reflections); RealVis is the most like a street photograph; all three pass on full-body proportions and hands.
  • N3 Pure scene (Q3): the three are close to tied, all leaning illustrative, and all handle the golden-dusk mood adequately.
  • N4 Instruction fidelity (Q4): only base got all three attributes right — purple elephant, striped hat, red balloon; both Juggernaut and RealVis changed the red balloon to yellow. Separately, no model used the elephant's trunk to "hold" the balloon — a shared, unscored observation.
  • N5 Speed: generation at 26–28 seconds per image is the same class for all three; loading times differ by nearly a factor of two (104 / 109 / 206 seconds). Saying "RealVis is slow" as if it generated slowly is misreading the table.

4. Selection Judgement (Codes and Priority Order)

For any selection scenario, take the first code that holds in the following priority order; each scenario takes exactly one code:

  1. H Hold the decision: the scenario explicitly states any of the following — (a) production will switch to the community customary parameters (cfg 5–6 plus a DPM++ 2M sampler) yet wants this round's ranking as the final ranking; (b) speed is the deciding basis, but the execution hardware is not this round's T4 class, or this round's speed scores are to be extrapolated to other hardware; (c) "RealVis generates the slowest" is given as the reason to eliminate it (contradicted by the N5 measurements). This round's evidence cannot support that decision; retest, or restate the claim, and re-judge.
  2. B Select SDXL base 1.0: the requirement explicitly demands that specified attributes (colours, objects, counts) be exact item by item, with any single miss counting as failure. Base has the highest instruction-fidelity score (9) and is the only model that passed Q4 in full.
  3. R Select RealVisXL V5.0: the requirement explicitly names "the most natural skin texture, the closest to a real photograph" as the top criterion. Under N1 and N2's qualitative observations, RealVis wins on that criterion; the composite does not override this judgement.
  4. J Select Juggernaut XL v9: every remaining scenario — portrait-led, full-body/scene-led, or general use. Juggernaut's portrait score (9) is the highest; where full-body/scene ties with RealVis at 9, the composite breaks the tie (8.25 > 7.5); general use takes the highest composite directly.

5. Honest Limits (Where This Ranking Applies)

  • Home parameters untested: this round ran on a unified specification (cfg 7); Juggernaut's and RealVis's community customary parameters are cfg 5–6 plus a DPM++ 2M sampler, and a rematch on their home parameters could flip the ranking — that is the next round's subject.
  • Speed is platform-bound: generation and loading figures were all measured on a free T4-class environment; changing hardware requires a retest, never extrapolation.
  • A single blind reviewer: the scores come from one reviewer (images seen without filename order), not a panel; they are editorial judgements, not laboratory measurements.
  • Next round preview: the two community models rematch the same prompts on their own customary parameters, and any newly released model gets a supplementary test as soon as it appears.

6. Common Mistakes Checklist

  • Choosing by composite alone: the composite averages four axes away, and the dimension you care about most may be exactly the winner's lowest score.
  • Reporting slow loading as slow generation: RealVis generates in the same class as the other two; it is slow to load. Only short, single-image sessions are hurt by loading; long batches amortise it.
  • Applying this ranking to home-parameter production: the ranking holds only for the unified specification; change the parameters and it is a different match.
  • Assuming a "smarter" community model will also be more obedient: this round's evidence points the other way — the better a model is at prettifying a picture, the more likely it is to quietly rewrite your specified attributes.
