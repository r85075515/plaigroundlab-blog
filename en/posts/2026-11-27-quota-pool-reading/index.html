Hit an AI Quota Limit? First Work Out Which Kind of Pool You Hit

For your AI: this post ships with a clean-Markdown agent edition — read it and follow along: English · 中文版

One "limit reached" message hides three completely different waits

When an AI tool stops mid-task with "limit reached," most people just wait — come back later, try again, and hope. But after measuring the accounts we actually use, pool by pool, it turns out there are three different kinds of pools: some refill on their own within hours, some reset only at a fixed cycle anchor, and some never reset at all — a drained balance is simply drained. The problem with blind waiting is not patience; it is not knowing which kind you hit.

We measured our own accounts pool by pool and maintain the results as a one-page AI Quota Reset Calendar. This post covers the reading method: how to identify a pool's type from what its interface shows, and what to do for each type. The per-pool reset times and the steps to find your own are on that page.

One caveat up front: every time quoted here and on the calendar page is a sample from our own accounts, read on 2026-11-26/27 (America/Phoenix). Your anchor depends on your account and subscription day — always go by what your own account shows.

Type 1: Rolling windows — you are waiting for your own usage to age out

A rolling window refills segment by segment, matched to when you used the quota: whatever you consumed at a given moment returns one window-length later. There is no single "reset day," only continuous refill.

  • Gemini App's 5-hour window: a 5-hour rolling window; usage returns segment by segment five hours later. Our sample: refill at 2026-11-27 03:07.
  • Muse's 7-day window: a per-account 7-day rolling window. Our seven accounts' reset times spread from 2026-11-27 16:13 to 2026-12-03 18:56 — same team, different anchor per account, which is exactly what per-account rolling looks like in practice.

What to do: don't wait for a full refill — schedule heavy work after the refill segments land. With multiple accounts, read each account's anchor and stagger the work; never assume the whole team recovers on the same day.

Type 2: Fixed anchors — the whole pool resets at one calendar point

Anchor pools do not refill gradually. The entire pool resets at a fixed calendar point: a set time each week, or a set day each month. The anchor usually follows your subscription or activation day and differs per account — it is not a universal Monday reset.

  • Gemini App's weekly limit: the second layer stacked on the 5-hour window. Our sample: resets 2026-12-01 06:07.
  • Grok (SuperGrok): since the 2026-11-18 redesign, Imagine and Chat share one weekly pool. Our sample: resets 2026-12-02 10:05.
  • Flow (AI Pro credits): a monthly credit pool, 1,000 credits a month, anchored to the subscription day. Our sample: balance 925 credits, next reset 2026-12-22 02:01 — anchor day 22, matching the subscription day.

What to do: put the anchor in your calendar and plan capacity around it. With this type, waiting a few hours refills nothing; retrying only burns attention.

Type 3: Balances — there is no reset, only credits and top-ups

Balance pools only go down. There is no "reset time" field at all. The balance rises in exactly two ways: periodic credits, or you topping up.

  • Gemini API (AI Studio): prepaid consumption with no periodic reset; the "monthly quota" is just the billing month's cost tally. Our sample: US$5.00 prepaid, US$3.97 spent this month, US$1.03 left.
  • Colab (AI Pro): Compute Units are a balance, deducted as you burn; AI Pro credits 200 units a month, on your subscription day. Our sample: balance 148.96 units, subscription day the 9th, next credit estimated 2026-12-09. Note that Colab's interface does not show the credit date — that date is an estimate derived from the subscription day, to be checked in subscription management, never quoted as a displayed fact.

What to do: monitor the balance and the credit date, not a reset countdown. If the balance can't cover the job, top up or wait for the credit before starting — don't schedule a long run that starves halfway.

The three most common misreads

  1. Using one pool's time for another: Grok Bot is a separate weekly pool from SuperGrok (our sample: 74% used, resets 2026-11-29 09:44). Projecting SuperGrok's Dec 2 onto the Bot is wrong by three days. Same brand does not mean same pool.
  2. Treating a two-layer product as one layer: Gemini App stacks a 5-hour window and a weekly limit — the short window refilling does not refill the weekly limit. You are fully stopped only when both are exhausted, and the short layer always recovers first.
  3. Quoting an estimate as an interface fact: a date the interface doesn't show (like Colab's credit date) can only be estimated from the subscription day and labeled as an estimate. Writing it as a confirmed date builds your whole schedule on a fake anchor.

Bottom line: classify first, then wait

The first move at a limit is not waiting — it is classifying. Look at what the interface offers: segment-by-segment refill (rolling), a single fixed point (anchor), or only a balance (balance type). Classify correctly and the wait turns from "unknown" into something you can put in a calendar. Full mechanisms, our samples, and how to check your own account for every pool are on the AI Quota Reset Calendar, updated weekly.
